# AD9361 receive gain table and TX attenuator, measured step by step (#29), 2026-10-14

Bench: board A TX1 -> 30 dB -> splitter -> board B RX1 (board A RX1 on another output), conducted,
3.84 MS/s. Board A transmits a 300 kHz tone (cyclic `iio_writedev`, 0.7 of full scale). The tone
level is the FFT bin of the tone in a gap-free 16384-sample capture, so noise and the DC offset do
not enter it. A point is used only if the tone is at least 30 dB above the median noise bin and the
ADC peak stays below 0.8 of full scale. Sweeps that need more range than the ADC window allows are
run at several levels and joined over their common valid points. Scripts:
`blocks/block_11_integrated_sdr_project/python/two_board_rx_gain_steps.py` and
`two_board_tx_atten_steps.py`. Raw data:
[`ad9361-gain-steps-20261014.json`](ad9361-gain-steps-20261014.json).

## Receive gain, manual, 0 to 73 dB in 1 dB steps

Board B's manual RX gain, swept at TX -5, -35 and -65 dB. The gain readback always equals the value
set. Received tone level relative to gain 0, in dB:

| gain set | 433 MHz | 915 MHz | 2400 MHz |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| 10 | 10.1 | 9.6 | 9.5 |
| 16 | 16.3 | 15.7 | 14.5 |
| 17 | 16.6 | 15.9 | 15.3 |
| 20 | 19.6 | 18.8 | 18.4 |
| 29 | 28.6 | 27.7 | 26.5 |
| 30 | 29.6 | 28.8 | **25.1** |
| 31 | 30.1 | **25.7** | 25.9 |
| 33 | **29.5** | 27.6 | 27.8 |
| 40 | 36.5 | 34.6 | 34.6 |
| 50 | 46.4 | 44.9 | 42.4 |
| 51 | 47.3 | 45.3 | 43.4 |
| 52 | **43.4** | **43.5** | 44.5 |
| 60 | 51.3 | 50.8 | 52.6 |
| 70 | 60.8 | 60.5 | 62.3 |
| 73 | 63.5 | 63.6 | 63.6 |

- **The table is not monotonic.** Around three settings the step is far from 1 dB, and two of them
  go backwards:
  - near 16-17 the step is 0.2-0.4 dB;
  - near 30-33 the gain *drops*: -3.1 dB at 915 MHz (30 -> 31), -1.7 dB at 433 MHz (32 -> 33),
    -1.4 dB at 2400 MHz (29 -> 30);
  - near 51-52 it drops again: -1.8 dB at 915 MHz, -3.9 dB at 433 MHz, -0.8 dB at 2400 MHz.

  The places are the same at all three frequencies and the sizes depend on the frequency. These
  are presumably the points where the gain table moves gain between stages (LNA, mixer, TIA).
  This is not checked against the AD9361 gain table itself.
- Elsewhere the steps are 0.7-1.35 dB.
- **The total is 63.5 dB for 73 nominal dB** (an average of 0.84-0.87 dB per step).
- At 2400 MHz the last two steps (72, 73) are flat: the table tops out there.
- At 915 MHz the two TX levels that both cover these points (-5 and -35 dB) give the same steps
  to within 0.3 dB (-3.1/-3.2 dB at 30 -> 31, -2.2/-2.2 dB at 51 -> 52), so they are not noise.

## TX attenuator, 0 dB down in 1 dB steps

Board A's TX gain swept from 0 dB down; board B at a fixed manual gain (10 dB for the strong end,
50 dB for the weak end).

| carrier | range measured | slope (dB received per dB set) | largest deviation from a straight line |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 915 MHz | 0 to -64 dB | 0.9999 | 0.42 dB |
| 2400 MHz | 0 to -67 dB | 0.986 | 0.76 dB |

The TX attenuator does what it says, to within a fraction of a dB.

## What it means for the labs

- To move the level by a known amount, step the **TX** gain, not the RX gain: the TX attenuator is
  accurate to about 0.5 dB over 60+ dB.
- Do not treat the RX gain setting as a calibrated dB scale. One step up can lower the level by
  up to 4 dB, near 31-33 and 52 dB.
- A level reading must be paired with the RX gain it was taken at, and compared only between
  measurements at the same setting.
- The AGC uses the same table; its large steps (for example 73 -> 60 dB in the #58 runs) cross
  these irregular points.
- Not measured: absolute levels (the S21 of the path is still missing), the AGC's internal table
  indices, frequencies other than 433/915/2400 MHz.
